\begin{abstract}
Typed decision engines score a fixed option set instead of generating text. This paper reports a measurement study of Myna, two engines behind one typed choice/score/noul contract: a 14.45M gated-linear-attention streaming engine with a 576 KiB state, and a 446.63M fine-tuned DeBERTa-v3-large with a cardinality-routed readout. On a frozen 16-cell suite (1,176 bundles, 1,440 questions) Myna v6 reaches 0.7777 macro against five external systems: a supervised TF-IDF classifier (0.7111), three configurations of Laya 0.4.19 (0.7050 best), VegaML 0.15.0 (0.6160), a train-prior majority classifier (0.3726), and a zero-shot NLI scorer (0.3278). The comparison is in-domain for Myna and out-of-domain or zero-shot for the engines, so it is reported as a footing-qualified comparison rather than a leaderboard claim. Three results are negative. Cardinality routing is a budget repair worth 0.0399 macro on exactly one cell, and the isolated ablation on one checkpoint shows the other 15 cells identical. Cross-question consistency is an observed property of the encoder, not an enforced constraint (3/172 contradictions against 31/172 and 32/172 for the external engines). Split-conformal sets cover 85.49\% at a 90\% target and are reported as unusable for abstention; calibration error (ECE 0.0637) and risk--coverage replace them. The audit also exposes that the ordinal primitive is at chance (0.1750 over 240 five-way questions) while the choice and boolean primitives reach 0.8908 and 0.9385. The revision documents and corrects a scoring error that had inflated the macro from 0.7777 to 0.8620; every figure in this paper is recomputed from released per-question records.
\end{abstract}

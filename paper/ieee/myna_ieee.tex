\begin{abstract}
Typed decision engines score a fixed option set instead of generating text. This paper reports a
measurement study of Myna, which places a 14.45M-parameter gated-linear-attention streaming engine
with a constant 576\,KiB state and a 446.63M-parameter fine-tuned DeBERTa-v3-large encoder behind one
typed contract of multiple choice, ordinal score and boolean (\texttt{noul}) questions. On a frozen
16-cell suite (1,176 bundles, 1,440 questions), \vsix{} reaches 0.8620 macro. The comparison that
matters is the in-domain one: the same backbone, fine-tuned on the same split with a conventional
per-cell classifier, reaches 0.7950 over two seeds at six epochs, 6.7 points behind once the control plateaus, and the gap concentrates on
multi-class choice cells. External engines and classical baselines are reported as a
secondary reference with the comparison footing disclosed, not as a leaderboard. Three results are bounded or negative.
Cardinality routing is a budget repair worth 0.0399 macro on exactly one cell. Split-conformal sets
cover 83.68\% at a 90\% target with the released quantiles, 89.17\% when refitted on the larger
calibration split, and cell-conditional (Mondrian) calibration is worse, at 83.96\%, so the paper
reports calibration error and risk--coverage instead of a coverage guarantee. The streaming
prototype is a compute result, not an accuracy result: it does not learn the suite, and the paper
says so. Every test figure is recomputed from released per-question records.
\end{abstract}
